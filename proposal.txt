Project Title: AI-Powered Spatial Omics Copilot — Bridging Tissue Visualization and Biomedical Intelligence

================================================================================

1. INDUSTRY / APPLICATION DOMAIN

Biomedical research and computational pathology. Specifically, the intersection
of spatial transcriptomics, digital pathology, and AI-assisted scientific discovery.

================================================================================

2. REAL-WORLD PROBLEM STATEMENT

Spatial transcriptomics technology can now capture gene expression across thousands
of locations within a single tissue sample, producing gigapixel histology images
paired with rich molecular data. However, interpreting this data requires simultaneous
expertise in pathology, molecular biology, and the published literature — a bottleneck
that slows research and limits accessibility.

The core problem: the data and the knowledge exist, but they are disconnected. A
researcher can visually identify a suspicious tissue region, but cannot instantly know
what genes are driving it, which pathways are involved, or what published studies have
said about similar findings. This gap forces researchers to context-switch across
multiple tools, manually cross-reference databases, and spend hours reading papers
before forming a biological interpretation.

Our project addresses this by building an AI copilot directly embedded in a spatial
omics visualization tool. Researchers simply draw a region of interest (ROI) on the
tissue, and the system immediately returns biologically grounded, literature-backed
interpretations — without leaving the visualization interface.

4. DESIRED OUTCOMES

By the end of the project we aim to deliver:

- An interactive visualization tool where users can load a whole-slide histopathology
  image, overlay gene expression data, and draw ROIs on tissue regions of interest.

- An AI copilot that, given a selected ROI, automatically:
    (1) identifies the top differentially expressed genes in that region,
    (2) maps them to relevant biological pathways, and
    (3) retrieves and summarizes relevant PubMed abstracts.

- An agentic RAG pipeline (LangChain/LangGraph + Ollama or Claude) that reasons
  over the spatial gene expression context and retrieved literature to produce a
  concise biological interpretation.

- A streaming chat interface embedded in the visualization tool so researchers can
  ask follow-up questions about their selected region in natural language.

The system will be evaluated on biological relevance of generated interpretations,
retrieval quality (precision of PubMed results), and user experience (response
latency, clarity of output).
